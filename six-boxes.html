Six-box method

Play behind, straddling, and in front of the root on the E and A strings. Connect the major scale, pentatonic, and triad in each position.

Connected fretboard

Robb Cappelletto’s six-position exercise, as demonstrated by Fret Science.

One key a day

Move through the six positions from the lower root to the higher one. In each, play the major scale → major pentatonic → major triad arpeggio, ascending and descending. That’s 18 patterns per key.

Behind: little finger on the root. Straddling: middle finger. In front: index finger, with three notes per string for the scale and a diagonal route for the pentatonic and triad.

How this relates to your five boxes

The E-root shapes use G-form and E-form; the A-root shapes use C-form and A-form. The two front shapes add 3NPS routes. The D-form is outside this exercise.

This view follows the lesson’s major-key routine. Its box numbers follow neck order in the selected key, so the A-root group comes first in keys such as D. Roots near the nut move up an octave when needed to keep the complete shapes playable.
