How CAGED fits

CAGED is five overlapping fretboard regions derived from movable C, A, G, E, and D chord forms. Each form gives you roots, thirds, and fifths for the chord you choose to map. Add scale notes around that chord skeleton.

A CAGED letter names the underlying chord form—not the key, mode, or first note. Every mode can be played in every usable CAGED region.

In the connected overview, P1–P5 are named for the parent-major chord form. Modal roots and tonic chords are a separate layer inside those same physical regions.

    Tonic chord landmarks

    A tonic triad stacks every other scale degree from home: 1–3–5. Add the next alternate degree for 1–3–5–7.

    • Major: 1 3 5
    • Minor: 1 ♭3 5
    • Diminished: 1 ♭3 ♭5

    These chord tones are strong phrase targets and the skeleton that makes CAGED useful.

    Relative vs. parallel modes

    Relative
    Same seven notes; tonic changes. Best for seeing how this shared map contains all seven modes.
    Parallel
    Same tonic; interval formula changes. Best for hearing what makes each mode distinct.

    Starting or ending on another note is not enough. Use a drone, bass, tonic chord, repeated emphasis, and resolution to establish the mode.

    Fretboard facts

    • Standard tuning, low to high: E A D G B E.
    • Strings move by fourths except G → B, which causes the familiar shape shift.
    • The same notes and geometry repeat 12 frets higher.
    • CAGED regions overlap; they are not sealed boxes.

    Five-step practice loop

    1. Hear it. Sustain the tonic with a drone or simple modal vamp.
    2. Anchor it. Find every 1; play 1–3–5–7.
    3. Phrase it. Use the major or minor pentatonic core.
    4. Color it. Add the identity cue deliberately against the tonic.
    5. Connect it. Cross into one neighbor and resolve to 1 or a chord tone.

    Common traps

    “This box is the mode.”
    The box is a location; tonic and harmony create the mode.
    “Begin on the root and it becomes modal.”
    A first note alone does not establish tonal center.
    “Move the same shape one fret for the next mode.”
    That transposes the same mode; it does not change its interval formula.
    “Pentatonic omissions are avoid notes.”
    The omitted notes frequently carry the modal identity.
    “Phrygian and Lydian need different regions.”
    They can share nearly the same frets while assigning different roots and intervals.
    “Seven modes require seven CAGED shapes.”
    CAGED has five regions; modes and common 3NPS systems each use seven for different reasons.
